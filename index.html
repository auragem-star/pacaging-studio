In plain words

What a dieline generator should give you.

A dieline is the flat drawing a die-cutter follows: where the knife cuts, where the board creases, and how far the artwork bleeds past the edge. Getting it wrong costs a plate, a press run, or a whole order of boxes.

Parametric, not a stock file

Most box dieline templates on the web are fixed PDFs at one size. These are generators: type a length, width, height and board caliper, and the flat is redrawn to those numbers.

19 structures: reverse tuck ends, auto-lock bottoms, a 1-2-3 bottom tuck top, self-locking flip tops, roll-end mailers, hinged lid trays, four- and six-pack bottle carriers, gable boxes with a handle, SOS paper bags, pillow boxes, popcorn boxes and two point-of-sale displays.

A file, not a picture of one

Every dieline exports as PDF, AI and SVG — vector, at true size, in millimetres. Nothing is rasterised and nothing is flattened, so the line work opens in Illustrator or a prepress tool the way it was drawn.

Cut, crease and bleed come out on separate layers in the production colours: #FF0000 cut, #0000FF crease, #00FF00 bleed.

Yours to set

The dimensions, the board caliper and the bleed are all yours, and you choose whether the numbers you type are internal, external or die-cut — so a size quoted to a customer and a size cut into steel do not have to be the same conversation.

It is a starting geometry, not a signed-off job. Proof a sample before you commit to plate or steel.
